Intake Procedures

Built from the unit's posted 6A Admission Check List, the typed 6A Admission Checklist, the unit's handwritten admission notes, the VAAES Mental Health NSG Admission Screen (Version 1.3), and a worked sample of the completed notes. Part 1 — Before the Patient Arrives

Pre-arrival to the unit. Providers put in orders once they accept — always check orders.

Consent is signed
Admission orders are in CPRS — including rescue meds
CSRE is completed by SW or provider
COVID test is negative
Check vitals and labs in ED
Check the chart — bring up concerns to the provider
See the 6A admission exclusion criteria — and admission criteria in general
Any additional equipment: CPAP, prosthetics, walker/wheelchair, fall risk, need medical bed
🍺 ETOH — The BAL Rule

BAL ≤ 299

For ETOH, the blood alcohol level has to be 299 or below. If above, the patient goes to medical, or gets fluids and a redraw to show ≤ 299.

💊 Orders — Rescue Meds & Detox

Rescue meds, both PO & IM, must be in place before the patient can enter the unit on off tours — 4:30 PM to 8 AM during the week, and on weekends.

Detoxing? Make sure there is a CIWA order, with PO & IM 1 mg & 2 mg orders of Ativan. Detoxing from fentanyl/opioids? Do they need a COWS / Bentyl / Zofran / Clonidine for detox? Check the COWS order set. And: outpatient meds?

📋 PHQ-9 / Columbia Scale

Has to be done if it is due in clinical reminders. Usually the Columbia is done by RNs in the ED — if positive, SW will be triggered to do the CSRE. For transfers from other facilities, we do the Columbia on the VAAES admission note — let SW know.

Part 2 — On Arrival to the Unit

From the ED call to a settled, charted admission.

1

ED calls — go get the patient

The ED will call when they are ready. Go down, get report from the ED nurse, and evaluate the Veteran. The IV needs to be out. Someone from the ED should accompany you and the Vet up to the unit.

2

Vitals — and issue their BP cuff

Obtain vital signs with height and weight. Issue a BP cuff — and write their name + room number on it when taking initial vitals.

3

Hygiene bag, 6A folder, and the Behavioral Agreement

As the patient acclimates, issue them a hygiene bag and explain its purpose — a good time to mention Oral Care, a required intake education item. Also give them a 6A folder. Cover the Rules of Behavior; have them sign the Behavioral Agreement form; turn that in to the unit clerk.

4

Skin assessment and hazardous-items search

2 6A staff (at least one an RN) do the skin assessment and search for hazardous items — make sure there are none. Look under underwear and socks. Change clothes — their clothes have to be washed.

5

Their belongings come up with them

When a patient is changed out of their street clothes at the ER, make sure that what they wore in (the white plastic bag) and their manila envelope of valuables come up to 6A. Any other extra items, like suitcases or other belongings, end up going to baggage.

6

Not on the 6A roster? Call the AOD

If the Veteran is not on the 6A roster, call the AOD.

7

Chart the admission set

Bring up the patient in CPRS and begin your paperwork on the Notes tab — the notes themselves are Part 3, in order: Screen first, then the Data Base, Behavioral Agreement Note, Patient Education, and Skin Inspection. The Plan of Care in the Mental Health Suite closes the set.

8

The blurb — Word, print, tape

Complete the short synopsis of the admission presentation and precipitants in Word, print it out, and tape it on NOK. The Admission Database carries the same text — the chief complaint as the blurb.

9

Verify orders — and the Kardex

Verify orders (sometimes the charge nurse does this). Then VISTA: Kardex — E for Edit, then E again: Admitting DX · Allergies · Diet · Advance Directive · *** and Flags, CIWA's, COWS, finger sticks, anything unusual, 1:1.

Part 3 — The Admission Checklist, In Order

The five CPRS notes in the order they run, then the Mental Health Suite closer. Tap the ring on any card for its detail popup — the ring fills as the set progresses, step by step.

🖥 In CPRS — Finding the Notes

Begin your paperwork on the Notes tab. Note 1 — the Screen: go to New Note and search for Admission Screen — the form “VAAES MENTAL HEALTH NSG ADMISSION SCREEN” will show in results; open and complete it. Note 2 — the Data Base: go back to New Note and search for Acute Care — the form “NURSING ACUTE CARE ADMISSION DATABASE, [patient name]” will show in results; open that note next. Notes 3–5 and the Mental Health Suite follow in the same sequence.

📌 The Order — Screen First

The CPRS forms run Screen → Data Base → Behavioral Agreement Note → Patient Education → Skin Inspection → Mental Health Suite. Older unit lists put the Data Base first and the Behavioral Agreement last — the completed-notes workflow runs the Screen first and the Behavioral Agreement third. Every list, flow, and tick-off in this guide follows the workflow order.

1

VAAES Mental Health NSG Admission Screen

Open this note first. The enterprise-standard admission screen (Version 1.3) — “the RN database.” In CPRS: New Note → search “Admission Screen” → open the “VAAES MENTAL HEALTH NSG ADMISSION SCREEN” form and work it top to bottom — all 27 sections in Part 5. If the Veteran is transferring from another facility, the Columbia scale must be done on this note — let Social Work know.

2

Nursing Acute Care Admission Data Base

The examination note of the admission. In CPRS: New Note → search “Acute Care” → open the “NURSING ACUTE CARE ADMISSION DATABASE, [patient name]” note. The header pulls the vitals in automatically; then work the systems in the note's own order — Neuro through Skin/Braden, IV sites, Organ Donor, Disposition of Belongings (Part 5). Per the unit's notes: use the chief complaint as the “blurb” in the Admission Database.

3

Behavioral Agreement Note

One chart entry under “Signed Agreement:” — the Veteran reviewed the behavioral agreement, denied questions or concerns, verbalized understanding, and signed the agreement. The paper side happens on arrival (Part 2): cover the Rules of Behavior, have the patient sign the Behavioral Agreement form, and turn that in to the unit clerk.

4

Patient Education

Chart the education with the note's standard text: the patient was given a tour of the unit, received the Welcome to 6A unit packet and Recovery handout, the packet/handout information was discussed, the patient was told to notify staff of any changes, concerns, or questions, universal fall precaution education was done, and the Veteran verbalized good understanding. Oral Care — a required intake education item — is covered on arrival when the hygiene bag is issued (Part 2).

5

VAAES Skin Inspection/Assessment

The skin note under its NEW name, charted from the two-staff inspection done on arrival (Part 2). Chart the inspection fields — skin color, temperature, moisture, turgor — the Braden Skin Assessment with its six subscales and total, interventions (needed or not needed), and skin integrity, and close with the line on hazardous items found / none found during the skin assessment completed with another RN. Cosign. (No Sepsis charting — that requirement is retired.)

6

Mental Health Suite — Care Plan

The closer. Complete the Plan of Care in the Mental Health Suite: select New Plan – Profile, go to each section and select what is appropriate. Build it from: 1 problem (nursing focused) · 1 goal “in their own words” · 1 objective · 4 or more interventions that are nursing focused, to be addressed daily.

📋 Riding Along — Clinical Reminders

The unit's sticky note scopes the charting set as “everything but Clinical Reminders” — but two duties still run through them at admission: make sure the COVID clinical reminder is completed, and the PHQ-9 / Columbia is done if it is due in clinical reminders (see Part 1).

Part 4 — Two Ways Into the Unit

How the patient arrives decides how much of the VAAES screen you complete.

Route A — Standard Admission

Open the VAAES Mental Health NSG Admission Screen first and work the full screen, every section, then the rest of the set in Part 3 order. Most admissions arrive through the ED — Columbia done by ED RNs; a positive triggers SW for the CSRE.

Route B — Transfer

For a transfer from an Acute Care unit: check "Patient Transferred From Acute Care Unit" at the top of the screen — then complete only the ten sections listed in Part 5.

For a transfer from another facility: the Columbia must be done on the VAAES admission note — and let SW know.

Part 5 — Inside the Notes, Section by Section

Every note in the set, walked in its own order — what gets entered, field by field. Reminder Dialog Template: VAAES Mental Health NSG Admission Screen, Version 1.3. Charting is in accordance with the VA Approved Enterprise Standard (VAAES) for Mental Health Nursing.

Note 1 — the full screen: all 27 sections, in the screen's own order:

1Allergy/Adverse Drug Reaction Review (MRT5) — remote allergy data (noted if none available) · facility allergy/ADR list · review conducted by: Nurse · confirm the result line: “Allergy/Adverse Drug Reaction list confirmed.”
2Medication Review (MRR1) — did the patient bring medication(s) from home? · medication review to be conducted by Provider.
3General Information — chief complaint (patient's own words) · admission information given by · legal guardian/conservator? · preferred language · preferred mode of communication · care-provider sex preference.
4Admission/Commitment Status — voluntary / involuntary · status documentation/forms reviewed by RN · copy placed in paper chart · scanned into EHR · patient expressing a desire or plan to leave? · wandering history · Patient Observation Status (e.g., 15-minute checks).
5Visitor Information — the patient's visitor restriction preferences.
6Environmental Safety Management — hazardous items check completed · patient oriented to unit · patient educated on ability to lock their bedroom door.
7Items at Bedside — items remaining with the patient (e.g., visual aids).
8Activities of Daily Living — hygiene ADLs: level of independence.
9Functional and Sleep — decline in function/independence in the last 6 months (+ describe) · sleeping behaviors, past 2 weeks · sleep practices, items, routines · usual hours of sleep · difficult to awaken from sleeping? · preference for how best to awaken (minimize startle).
10Pain Assessment — currently in pain? · DVPRS scale used · location · DVPRS descriptor + pain score · patient's acceptable pain goal · progress toward goal? · primary pain: type · describe pain (quality) · pain interventions.
11Morse Fall Scale & TIPS Program — history of falling (immediate or within 3 months) · secondary diagnosis · ambulatory aid · IV therapy/heparin lock · gait/transferring · mental status · total score + risk statement.
12Advance Directive — notification of rights: written notification provided · patient reports having a Mental Health Advance Directive · wishes information/assistance with Advance Care Planning/Advance Directive?
13Legal Issues — current legal issues.
14Infectious Disease Risk Screen — travel within US, last 21 days · travel outside US, last 21 days · exposure, last 14 days · other exposure to infectious disease · symptoms reported · history of MDRO.
15Nutrition Screening — malnutrition screening: weight, previous 6 months (auto-pulled measurement) · lost weight recently without trying (points) · eating poorly due to decreased appetite (points) · total score · teeth concerns · overeating to feeling sick/vomiting · gained 10 lbs+ in past 3 months without trying · food allergies/intolerance/special dietary needs/cultural-religious preferences · Food Insecurity Screening (2 questions, past 12 months).
16Spirituality — religious practices / spiritual concerns for the chaplain, provider, or team?
17Risk Screenings — Alcohol Screen (to be completed by Provider): consumes alcohol? · history of alcohol withdrawal? · Tobacco Use: status · alternative nicotine products? · Substance Use Assessment: recreational drugs or narcotics — prescription or non-prescription?
18Suicide Screen — the C-SSRS was completed within 24 hours prior to admission (confirm and document).
19Risk and Protective Factors — risk factors (e.g., history of mental health hospitalization + describe) · protective factors and reasons for living (checklist + comment field).
20Violence Risk Screen — violence to others (past 6 months / prior) · to property/objects (past 6 months / prior) · ever violent in an inpatient hospital setting · current thoughts of violence · current thoughts of homicide · actions taken.
21Exposure to Violence and Abuse Pre-Screen — worried for your safety / will be hurt or harmed? · anyone tried to force you to sign papers or use your money against your will?
22Stressors and Support — stressors · emotional support available? · screened for traumatic life experiences (acknowledges/denies).
23Strengths and Abilities — housing/residential stability · cultural/spiritual/religious beliefs and practices · employed or has income/benefits · expressed desire/motivation for change · interpersonal relationships and supports.
24Restraint/Seclusion Safety & Prevention — previously required restraint/seclusion? · ever placed in restraints (denies/confirms) + coping skills to maintain control · pre-existing medical conditions/disabilities raising restraint risk · history of sexual/physical abuse raising psychological risk · patient informed restraint/seclusion may be needed · patient given restraint/seclusion information · permission for family/significant other notification if restrained · Standard Release of Information form obtained?
25Traumatic Brain Injury History — history of TBI?
26Educational Needs/Learning Style — barriers to learning · learning style preferences: 1:1 · Demonstration · Group · Printed materials · Verbal explanation · Video/education TV.
27Anticipated Discharge Needs — patient's goal for discharge · where do you live (housing status) · method of transportation upon discharge · anticipated barriers to discharge.

Details That Matter

Fields marked * are required. The screen files under Health Factors: VA-VAAES Mental Health NSG Admission Screen V1.3 and the VA-VAAES patient transfer factor. The unit's notes also group the screen's frequent documentation together: VAAES — Frequent Doc, CIWA, COWS.

Note 2 — the Admission Data Base: header, arrival block, and systems, in the note's own order:

Header — auto-pulled — age · sex · vitals with date/time stamps: temperature · pulse · respirations · pulse ox · blood pressure · pain · height · weight.
Arrival, Handoff & Contacts — patient came to the unit/facility by (mode) · hand-off: admission · source of information · marital status · emergency contact · Primary Next of Kin Information (name · relationship to patient · address · phone).
Overall Appearance — how the patient presents on arrival.
Main Concern — “What is your main concern that brought you to the hospital today?” — in the patient's own words (this text feeds the blurb).
Neuro — mental status/orientation · memory · pupils (PERLA) · swallowing difficulty · speech.
EENT — eyes · ears · mouth and throat · nose — complaints or none.
Respiratory — respirations (depth/pattern) · breath sounds · productive cough · cough > 3 weeks? · cough + symptom cluster (weight loss etc.) · uses oxygen at home? · uses C-PAP machine?
Cardiovascular — pulses (regular/strength; pedal palpable; radial palpable) · pacemaker? · ICD? · edema · on anticoagulant therapy?
Musculoskeletal — range of motion · ambulation · tremors · injury? · patient quote field · comments (recent falls/accidents).
GI — abdomen · bowel sounds · change in bowel habits? · last BM · GI problems.
GU — voiding.
Psychosocial — behavior · employment status.
Skin / Mucus Membranes / Peripheral — Braden Scale: Sensory Perception · Moisture · Activity · Mobility · Nutrition · Friction (subscores, total, risk band 19–23 = No Risk) · skin description (intact · warm/dry · turgor · color) · pressure ulcer present? · skin/wound/pressure ulcer interventions.
IV Sites / Infusions — central line · peripheral line · dialysis line — present / not present.
Organ Donor — Is the Veteran an Organ Donor? Yes / No + reason (e.g., disease process).
Disposition of Belongings — what came up with the patient (e.g., bag(s) brought to the unit from the ED).

🔎 Note 5 — VAAES Skin Inspection/Assessment

Completed with a second RN. Chart, in order:

  • Skin Inspection: skin color · skin temperature · skin moisture · skin turgor
  • Braden Skin Assessment: the six subscales, each with its descriptor line — Sensory Perception · Moisture · Activity · Mobility · Nutrition · Friction and Shear — plus the total score and risk statement (a total of 23 = not at risk for development of pressure ulcers/injuries)
  • Interventions: pressure injury interventions — needed / not needed (patient not at risk)
  • Skin Integrity: e.g., intact
  • Closing line: hazardous items found / none found during the skin assessment completed with another RN

Chart the note with the hazardous-items result — cosign.
