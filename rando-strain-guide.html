🧭 How to use this guide & the evidence labels

Fourteen secondhand bags, photographed in my collection. Some name a vendor outright; most are hand-marked on a generic template. Every card states its attribution and its evidence level in the open — read those first.

🧭

Why “Rando”: these bags do not belong to any vendor guide already published in this series — they are not Tree Leaf, not DTE, not Phantom, and not any other profiled vendor’s confirmed product. They are the remainder of the shelf, and they get a guide of their own rather than being forced into someone else’s.

The labels used on every card: CONFIRMED — the vendor or product is directly attested. PROBABLE — the evidence points one way and is stated with its basis. POSSIBLE — a family resemblance only. Unattributed — said plainly where the research caps there; several sellers were never found, and that result stands as the finding. Class-level profile — the chart and the effect notes describe the strain name or class across vendors, not this bag, and the card says so.

The charts: tap the circular chart button on any card for its effect split across Energy, Mood, Clarity, Calm and Relief. Where a vendor published tester scores (the two That One Place bags), the chart is those scores converted, and the card says so. Where research found no effect reports at all — Glow and Blue Flame — the chart is an equal split, labeled on the card as a no-claim placeholder. An honest blank is worth more than an invented profile.

The generic template family: most of the unattributed bags share one printed template — a vein line and a size ladder with the choices underlined in pen, and the name hand-written. That template family is tied, at the levels stated on each card, to Smiley Time Herbals and its era of 28 g sample packs. The template can support a family reading; it cannot name the person who handed me any single bag.

⚪ White bags — clean drive & focus

Three whites, three different evidence stories: a commodity strain name with a strong class record, a name with vendor copy only, and a confirmed private batch with a full tester profile.

⚪

The white section holds this guide’s clearest lesson: the same color can carry a strong record, a thin one, or a vendor’s own — and the cards keep those separate.

White Kali “06”

Unattributed · White · ~40 g
📦 ~40 g🏷️ Vendor: Unattributed🔎 Strain class: strong cross-vendor record

🌿 The leaf

“Kali” is Kalimantan — the Indonesian part of Borneo — in standard trade usage, so this is a white-vein Kalimantan-style leaf. Nothing on the bag marks it as a blend: the “White” option is the underlined one. The “06” matches no vendor numbering habit found in research; it reads most plausibly as the secondhand seller’s own lot shorthand.

📡 What the research found

The bag sits on the generic template family tied at POSSIBLE to Smiley Time Herbals — the 40 g size matches that vendor’s current sample-pack unit, and its current list does carry a Kali, but only a green one (“OG Green Kali”). No White Kali listing, current or archived, was found there. White Kali is a commodity strain name sold by many unrelated vendors, so even a perfect name match buys no vendor attribution for this bag.

💬 Community reports — the strain class

Reports for this bag: none. For the named strain across vendors, the consensus is unusually consistent for a white — energy first, focus close behind, a positive mood lift, a smoother-than-average calm edge, and relief as the weak axis. One named-vendor tasting called it “the most unpredictable strain I’ve sampled” — positive overall, with unpredictability as the headline.

“White Kali gives an energy boost without the jitters of coffee, but is still subdued in nature.”— Kratom Science, white strains index — on the White Kali class, not this bag

A daytime white on the class record: taken, in kratom.org’s words, as people take coffee — for revitalization and increased focus.

None found. No Reddit thread and no independent review of White Mountain was located; the named vendor’s own product page shows no customer reviews. The profile on this card is vendor description only — energy, mood and focus as the three headline traits — and no calm or relief claim is made, because none was found.

“White Mountain is a white-vein powder grown in the highland zones of Borneo (Kalimantan), where cooler nights slow leaf maturity.”— Hudson Valley Botanicals, White Mountain product page — vendor copy, labeled as such

The one scored reception located anywhere is an on-site 4.80/5 from 10 reviews for the “Super” variant at a different vendor — its individual review texts could not be retrieved, so it is noted here and not built on.

🟡 Golds & yellows — mellow warmth

Yellow and gold are made, not grown: post-harvest drying, curing and fermentation turn standard leaf into the mellow, mood-forward family. All three bags here live in that processed world — including the one labeled with a color that does not grow at all.

🟡

Only red, green and white veins grow on the tree. Everything in this section is process and naming — which is exactly why its cards lean on method and class evidence rather than vein claims.

Yellow Vietnam

Unattributed · Yellow · 28 g
📦 28 g🏷️ Seller: Unattributed🔎 Evidence: strong strain-class record

🌿 The leaf

“Yellow” is a process, not a vein — there is no naturally yellow-veined leaf. Yellow leaf is made after harvest, by extended drying and sun-curing, by fermentation, or by blending vein colors, and no industry standard governs which method a given yellow used; which one produced this bag is unknown. “Vietnam” is printed here as the strain name, not a verified growing origin: sourcing commentary across the trade treats it as a style name, with the leaf generally Indonesian-grown.

📡 What the research found

The bag is on the older revision of the generic template family, at PROBABLE for that family. The small “2” after the name is unresolved — a count or inventory mark by whoever bagged it is the most plausible reading, labeled inference. The seller of this bag is Unattributed.

💬 Community reports — the strain class

Mood is the class signature: warm, golden mood elevation, reported again and again, with calm focus, gentle energy, and mild-to-moderate relief around it. Onset is repeatedly described as slower than most strains, and duration commonly around 3–5 hours. The dissent is part of the record and kept: at least one reviewer found it simply did not do much, and yellow’s non-standard production makes the whole category vary more between vendors than the standard vein colors.

“The yellow Vietnam I have is my favorite strain right now. More energetic than a white and highly euphoric.”— KayLaDeL, Reddit — quoted in KratomKrush’s Yellow Vietnam review; on the strain class

“Meh. It just doesn’t do much for me. Reds and Whites are my go-to strains.”— queenbeepdx, Reddit — quoted in the same review roundup; the dissent, kept on the record

“This was the best Yellow Vietnam I’ve had. It’s very smooth, has great mood lift, provided comfort and a slight energy boost but I was still able to relax if I wanted to.” — a Mount Kratom customer review of its own Yellow Vietnam, quoted in research.

THAI MD

Unattributed · Gold · 40 g
📦 40 g🏷️ Seller: Unattributed🔎 Reading: Thai Maeng Da, gold-processed — PROBABLE as marking intent

🌿 The leaf

“THAI MD” is Thai Maeng Da — the reading is confirmed by trade shorthand, and the exact full form “Thai MD (Thai Maeng Da)” is attested as a product name at DTE Botanicals. The gold underline is best read the way this template family works: the name says which leaf, the underline says which category it was sold as — here, the gold category: Maeng Da leaf given gold processing (extended drying and/or a shorter fermentation than red). The leaf’s actual production method cannot be verified from the label. Thai-style leaf in the modern trade is almost certainly Indonesian-grown; that is inference from the universal trade pattern, stated as such.

📡 What the research found

No product named Thai Maeng Da, Gold Thai, or Gold Maeng Da could be verified in the template vendor’s catalog — a dead end, on the record. What matters for the profile is that both available signposts point the same way, away from the classic fast Thai green: gold processing pushes a Maeng Da toward the mellow end, and the one exact-name attestation is itself a slow, mellow product.

💬 Community reports — the name class

DTE rates its Thai MD “Mid to Mid-Low” — evening territory — and the Gold Maeng Da class agrees: calm and euphoric mood lead, clarity arrives soft rather than sharp, and relief reads moderate-to-good in class reports, with longer reported duration than green Maeng Da. The vendor’s own caveat is kept: it did not hit for him on the first run, and took two or three tries to shine.

“It’s got that old-school “slow green meets smooth red” vibe, with a very unique body glow and a relaxing, euphoric headspace. Great for mellow evenings or turning down the dial when life’s got you revved up.”— DTE Botanicals, Vintage Release — on its own Thai MD (Thai Maeng Da)

Do not profile this bag from classic fast green Thai material — the gold marking and the exact-name attestation both point toward the slow end.

Orange Vein Maeng Da

EntheoFarm · Maeng Da · 50 g
📦 50 g🏷️ Vendor: EntheoFarm (label)🔎 Product name: label-only attestation

🌿 The leaf

Plain leaf powder on the label’s evidence — M. speciosa, 50 grams, no extract or enhanced wording — from a real, currently operating small-batch vendor in Central Texas, operating since 2009 by its own account. The core finding is about the color word: there is no natural orange vein. Kratom leaves develop three natural vein colors — red, green and white — and every in-between market color (yellow, gold) is made after harvest, by extended drying, fermentation or curing, or by blending across the standard colors.

📡 What the research found

“Orange Vein Maeng Da” appears nowhere else: no catalog page at the vendor (whose current Maeng Da is Red, White Reserve, and Green “M4”), no other vendor listing, no review, no community mention, and no COA. Inference, labeled: a house name for an in-between Maeng Da made by one or more of the yellow/gold mechanisms — the vendor does use house-coded names elsewhere in its catalog. No recipe, ratio or process for this product was found, and none is asserted.

💬 Community reports

None for this product, and none for any “orange vein” product’s effects. The profile on this card is therefore built — and labeled — on the closest grounded analogue, Yellow/Gold Maeng Da: mood the most consistent axis, gentle attentiveness and focus, mild calm alongside, and relief entering mainly through whatever red-side share a blend or cure carries. Gold-class reports also split honestly in both directions on energy, by person and batch.

“I have a gold MD strain, and to me it’s between a white and red, as well as a little more relaxing than a green. Usually I’ll have some of the gold after lunch and it doesn’t slow me down or anything at work.”— Reddit user Sk1dmark82, as reproduced in KratomKrush’s Gold Maeng Da review — the analogue, not this product

The only other “orange vein” trace in community history is a skeptical complaint about a different, unrelated vendor’s claim — no connection to this bag was found, and the two are not merged here.

The greens are more mood elevation and pain relief while the reds are more sedating with pain relief. Red Maeng Da is an exception to the rule.”— Community commenter Tina, KratomScience — on Maeng Da and the veins in general, not this named lot

The interest of this bag is exactly its obscurity: the Maeng Da style is well documented — this named lot is documented by its label alone.

🧪 Alkaloid & lab reference

What is claimed, what was retrieved, and what was not found — kept separate.

🧪

No bag-specific COA was located for any of the fourteen bags in this guide. No lot or batch number appears on the generic-template bags, and no certificate for a named lot of any product here was retrieved in research. Any testing statement below is a vendor’s claim about its process, presented as a claim — not a verified result for a bag in my collection.

EntheoFarm (Kuno Jiwa Maeng Da, Orange Vein Maeng Da): the vendor’s homepage states, “We test in-house every strain and blend you’ll find here.” In-house is the vendor’s own word. No third-party COA for any EntheoFarm Maeng Da lot was located.

Tribe Botanicals (Super Green): the bag prints “Lab Tested,” and the vendor states every batch is third-party tested for purity, alkaloid content and contaminants, with results available. No COA tied to this bag’s lot was retrieved, and no lot number appears on the bag front.

That One Place (White Panda, Green Malay): the vendor describes a general process — lab tests and a strict testing protocol, with in-house and external testers, before a product is listed. No batch COA for either product was located. The pouches themselves make no lab-test claim on their fronts.

The generic-template bags (Glow, White Kali, White Mountain, Pmd, Yellow Vietnam, THAI MD, Blue Flame, Monster Y25, Silvia Dragon): no COA, no batch number, no lab figure of any kind was found for any of them. For the template family’s probable vendor, an independent review’s standing criticism is that it provides no batch numbers at all — which is precisely why these bags’ contents cannot be tied to any tested lot.

The one number that frames all of the above: the figure most COAs report is mitragynine, the leaf’s dominant alkaloid. In the best comparative evidence located in this research — a peer-reviewed survey of 644 users (Huisman et al., 2023) — users did report distinct experiences for red, green and white products in line with common descriptions, while independent lab analysis of those products found no significant cross-strain differences in alkaloid content. Reported character running ahead of measurable difference is the documented state of the whole category, and it is doubly true for names with no standard meaning at all.
