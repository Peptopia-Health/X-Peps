What Is the Wolverine Blend?
Two repair peptides in a single vial — the GLOW formula with the copper peptide left out, aimed at deep tissue instead of skin.

Wolverine is the research-market name for a fixed blend of BPC-157 and TB-500 — the two most-studied healing peptides, lyophilized together in one vial.

The pairing is a division of labor. BPC-157 works the local repair signal: in animal and cell studies it switches on new blood-vessel growth and makes repair cells more responsive to growth signals at the injury site. TB-500 works the logistics: it regulates actin, the structural protein cells use to move, so repair cells can migrate into damaged tissue. One peptide raises the signal, the other moves the crew — that is the entire logic of the blend, and the reason it is the healing-family counterpart to GLOW, which adds GHK-Cu for skin and collagen on top of the same two-peptide core.

Neither compound is an approved drug, and the blend itself has never been through a human trial. What exists is a deep animal literature for each peptide separately, a handful of small human reports for BPC-157, and a large body of published protocol practice. This guide keeps those three layers separate throughout.

BPC-157 — the local signal

A synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide (sequence GEPPPGKPADDAGLV) modeled on a protective protein found in human gastric juice. In rat tendon fibroblasts it upregulates the growth hormone receptor and activates the JAK-2 pathway (Chang 2014), and in endothelial models it raises VEGFR2 and switches on the VEGFR2–Akt–eNOS chain that builds new capillaries (Hsieh 2017). It also modulates the nitric oxide system (Sikiric 2014).

TB-500 — the migration crew

A synthetic fragment of thymosin beta-4, a 43-amino-acid protein found in nearly every cell. TB-500 is its 7-amino-acid actin-binding region (Ac-LKKTETQ, residues 17–23). By binding G-actin, thymosin beta-4 controls the cytoskeleton cells need to crawl — and in animal wound models it speeds cell migration into the wound bed and accelerates closure (Malinda 1999).

Why they are paired

Tendons and ligaments heal slowly largely because they are poorly supplied with blood. BPC-157's angiogenesis addresses the supply problem; TB-500's actin regulation addresses the movement problem. The mechanisms are complementary on paper and in animal models — the honest limit is that published synergy data for the pair is thin, and no controlled study has shown the blend beating either peptide alone.

The human record, stated in full: a 2025 systematic review of BPC-157 in orthopaedic sports medicine screened 544 articles and included 36 studies — 35 preclinical and 1 clinical (Vasireddi 2025).

Across both peptides, the entire published human record for BPC-157 is three small uncontrolled reports totaling about 30 people — the knee series above, a 12-person clinic series, and a 2-person intravenous safety pilot in which doses up to 20 mg produced no changes in cardiac, liver, kidney, thyroid, or glucose markers. For TB-500, the FDA's 2026 briefing states the agency "has not identified any human exposure data" for the fragment by any route. There is no randomized controlled trial of BPC-157, of TB-500, or of the blend, for any use. Every protocol in the next section is therefore published practice, not trial-derived dosing.

Dosing: Two Schedules in One Vial
The blend's central tension — BPC-157 is a daily microgram peptide, TB-500 is a load-and-maintain milligram peptide — and the three ways published protocols resolve it.

The two peptides do not naturally share a schedule, and no published human pharmacokinetic study exists for either one to settle the question.

BPC-157 is dosed in micrograms, once or twice a day — a small, frequent signal. TB-500 is dosed in milligrams, a few times a week — per injection roughly ten times the BPC-157 amount, front-loaded for 4–6 weeks and then maintained at a lower frequency. Weekly totals land closer than the per-dose gap suggests: about 4–5 mg of TB-500 per week during loading against 1.4–3.5 mg of BPC-157 per week. A pre-mixed vial forces one rhythm on both, so blend protocols are compromises — and the compromise each one chooses is visible in its numbers.

The weekly math shows what the blend trades away.

At 250 mcg of each daily, the blend delivers 1.75 mg of TB-500 per week — below its standalone loading range and roughly at maintenance level. At 500 mcg of each daily, TB-500 reaches 3.5 mg per week, approaching loading totals but spread evenly instead of pulsed. Reproducing a true TB-500 loading phase through the blend would mean about 1 mg of each per day — which simultaneously pushes BPC-157 to the top of its reported range. Protocols that want a genuine loading phase therefore keep the peptides in separate vials: BPC-157 at 250–500 mcg daily alongside TB-500 at 2 mg twice weekly, stepping down to 2 mg weekly. The blend's offer is one injection, one vial, no arithmetic — at maintenance-level TB-500 exposure.

Practical notes common across the published protocols: subcutaneous injection, near the injured area for local goals or into abdominal fat for systemic effect; rotate sites; a fresh syringe for every injection; cycle lengths of 4–8 weeks are typical (up to 12 for long-standing problems), followed by 2–4 weeks off.

Side Effects & Cautions
What the small human record shows, what regulators flag, and the one theoretical question the literature keeps open.
What has been reported

Two of the three small BPC-157 clinic reports recorded no adverse events; the third did not describe any. Reports that size cannot detect uncommon or delayed harms. In community and clinic use, the recurring complaints are headache, nausea, dizziness, fatigue, and injection-site redness or itching — anecdotal, with no measured rates for either peptide.

What regulators flag

The FDA's compounding reviews cite immunogenicity (immune reactions to injected peptides), impurity and endotoxin characterization gaps, and insufficient clinical safety information. In animal toxicology published by BPC-157's developers, no lethal dose was identified and no organ toxicity appeared at tested doses — while reviewers note that most of the BPC-157 literature originates from a single research group, with limited independent replication.

The angiogenesis question

A peptide that grows blood vessels invites an obvious question. The lab record is context-dependent: pro-angiogenic in injured tissue, yet in a tumor-cell-line study BPC-157 blunted VEGF-driven signaling. 2026 reviews list possible cancer promotion among theoretical, unmeasured concerns. The plain position: long-term human safety is unknown, there are no safety data in pregnancy, and neither peptide has been studied in children.

September 2023. The FDA placed both substances in Category 2 of its 503A bulk-substances framework — significant safety risks; compounding pharmacies could not use them.

April 2026. The FDA removed 12 peptides, including both compounds, from Category 2; their nominations were recorded as withdrawn. Removal is not authorization — it ended the explicit prohibition designation without placing either substance on the approved bulks list.

July 23, 2026. The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 8–6, with one abstention, to recommend adding BPC-157, KPV, and TB-500 to the 503A bulks list. FDA staff had recommended against, citing inadequate chemical characterization, impurity data, and clinical safety information. The vote is advisory only.

October 2026. Compounding was reported to be proceeding under FDA enforcement discretion while formal rulemaking remains pending — a policy posture that can change, not an approval. The FDA's own pages are the live reference for where this stands.

Storage & Handling
Peptides are fragile proteins in miniature — cold, dark, and gentle handling keeps a vial honest to the last dose.

🧊 Unopened Vial

Lyophilized powder keeps best refrigerated and away from light; vendor guidance for the blend is that both the powder vial and the water live in the fridge, before and after mixing. Keep the vial sealed until the day it is mixed.

💧 After Mixing

Refrigerate at 2–8 °C and use within about 30 days in common practice. Never freeze a reconstituted vial, and discard solution that turns cloudy, discolored, or shows particles.

🖐 Handling Rules

Swirl, never shake. Fresh sterile syringe for every draw and every injection; rotate injection sites to avoid nodules; keep the stopper swabbed and the mixed vial labeled with its date and concentration.
