Phase I Metabolism: CYP450 Oxidation

Upon oral ingestion, mitragynine undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism [cite: 2, 17]. The CYP3A4 enzyme is the dominant biological catalyst responsible for clearance [cite: 2, 19]. When CYP3A4 is inhibited (via compounds like ketoconazole), systemic exposure to both mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine scales by up to 130% [cite: 2, 23]. Therefore, safely inhibiting CYP3A4 represents the most effective pharmacological target for extending duration [cite: 25].

Auto-Regulation: Mitragynine and corynantheidine are potent, competitive inhibitors of the CYP2D6 enzyme [cite: 4, 27]. Thus, Kratom acts as its own potentiator for any concurrent compound that relies on CYP2D6 for clearance [cite: 14, 28].

Phase II Metabolism & Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB)

Surviving alkaloids undergo glucuronidation via UGT enzymes, rendering them hydrophilic for renal excretion [cite: 10, 15, 30, 31, 32, 33]. To truly potentiate, exogenous UGT inhibitors trap active alkaloids in the bloodstream [cite: 17, 35, 36]. At the BBB, a P-glycoprotein (P-gp) efflux pump continuously ejects alkaloids back into systemic circulation [cite: 20, 42]. Administering targeted P-gp inhibitors cripples this pump, trapping alkaloids in the central nervous system [cite: 19, 44].

Agmatine Sulfate: The Apex Neuromodulator

Agmatine sulfate is the gold standard for potentiation and tolerance management [cite: 55]. Magnesium (Glycinate or Citrate)
