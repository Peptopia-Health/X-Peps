🔤 The coded GLP names — decoder

Several products use coded names. Cross-referencing Freedom Diagnostics COAs published by other vendors:

  • GLP TZ / TIRX = tirzepatide. A Freedom Diagnostics COA for another vendor identifies "Tirzepatide (B12)/GLP-TZ" — the same code Daisy uses (99.88%, lot 06292026).
  • GLP RT / RETX = retatrutide (inference from the naming pattern). Retatrutide is Eli Lilly's investigational triple-agonist — not FDA-approved.
  • SEMX = semaglutide (inference: SM code; matches "GLP SM" on a Freedom COA for another vendor).
  • CARG = cagrilintide (inference: standard gray-market abbreviation).
  • KLOW = unidentified. Maps to no standard peptide abbreviation; possibly a house blend name. Only the vendor can say.

Coded names are consistent with evading keyword-based enforcement sweeps and payment-processor filters. Eli Lilly has sent cease-and-desist letters to US peptide vendors selling tirzepatide/retatrutide (community report).

⚖️ Legal status — the facts

The business model

Research peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu, ipamorelin, etc.) are unscheduled — not controlled substances — but are not FDA-approved for human use. The "for laboratory and research use only, not for human or veterinary consumption" labeling is the industry-standard gray-area model. Tirzepatide is FDA-approved (Mounjaro/Zepbound), so unapproved versions implicate Lilly's IP and drug-approval law; retatrutide is investigational and unapproved.

The enforcement landscape (2026)

  • United States v. Kawa (July 2026): the Paradigm Peptides owner was sentenced to 70 months in federal prison for introducing unapproved drugs into interstate commerce with intent to defraud — marketing products as "research use only" while knowing they were for human consumption, forging COAs, and ignoring prior FDA warning letters.
  • FDA, August 2026: warning letters to direct-to-consumer peptide sellers marketing GLP-1s and other peptides — described as "casting a wide net."
  • FDA, March 2026: 30 warning letters to telehealth companies over compounded GLP-1 marketing.

Daisy Peptides specifically — the unlicensed-pharmacy problem

No FDA warning letters, seizures, or lawsuits naming Daisy Peptides were found. Their site uses research-use language throughout and makes no therapeutic claims on the pages reviewed. That is the defense. Here is the prosecution:

  • They sell the active ingredient of a prescription drug without being a pharmacy. Tirzepatide is Mounjaro/Zepbound. Daisy sells it as "TIRX" / "GLP TZ" to anyone with a credit card. No prescription, no pharmacist, no medical license — a commercial unit in Las Vegas doing the work of a pharmacy with none of the oversight.
  • They sell an unapproved investigational drug. Retatrutide ("RETX" / "GLP RT") is not FDA-approved for anything. Selling it at retail — coded or not — is not a gray area with a long tradition; it is a new compound moving through unregulated channels.
  • The coded names are evasion behavior. TIRX, RETX, SEMX, "GLP TZ," "GLP RT" — this is the naming convention of sellers dodging keyword enforcement and payment-processor filters. Legitimate research suppliers do not need code names; they use CAS numbers.
  • The "researcher" customer is a fiction. Real laboratories buy through established chemical suppliers with institutional accounts — not via a two-month-old WordPress site with a Gmail-style contact address. The coded GLP products are the bestsellers of the gray market for one reason: humans inject them. Everyone in the chain knows it. The Kawa prosecution established that the DOJ knows it too.
  • No safety screening on any product. Purity and identity only — no endotoxin, no sterility, no heavy metals. For compounds people inject, that is not a gap; it is negligence with a storefront.
Bottom line: the RUO model is the standard gray area — until it isn't. The moment a prosecutor decides the "research use only" label is a fiction — and the coded names, the GLP catalog, and the credit-card checkout make that case easy to argue — this becomes United States v. Kawa with a different defendant. Seventy months. Nothing found puts Daisy outside the standard gray-area model yet — but a 2-month-old shop selling coded, unapproved GLP compounds from a shared commercial unit, with no legal entity and no named human, is not a compliance-forward profile. It is a raid waiting for a reason.
