What It Is

A Small Molecule, Not a Peptide

SLU-PP-332 is not a peptide. It is a small synthetic molecule — no amino-acid chain — first described in 2023. It is sold alongside peptides, which is why it sits in peptide catalogs, but chemically it belongs to a different family altogether.

It switches on the estrogen-related receptors (ERRs) — proteins inside the cell that turn whole groups of metabolism genes on and off. Despite the name, these receptors do not respond to estrogen. No natural hormone is known for them, which is why scientists call them orphan receptors.

One Molecule, Three Receptors

SLU-PP-332 is a pan-agonist: it activates all three ERRs — ERRα, ERRβ and ERRγ — and it is most potent at ERRα, the receptor that matters most in skeletal muscle. Genetic research had already tied the ERRs to how muscle builds exercise capacity; this molecule was the first practical way to switch them on.

How It Works

Gene Switches for Mitochondria and Fat Burning

1 · Switch

SLU-PP-332 binds the ERRs inside the cell — gene switches, not surface receptors — with its strongest action at ERRα.

2 · Program

The activated receptors turn on the same gene program a bout of aerobic exercise triggers: more mitochondria, more fat burned for fuel.

3 · Muscle

In mice, muscle shifted toward oxidative Type IIa fibres and the animals ran longer — an effect that disappeared when ERRα was removed, proving the switch is what does the work.

What the Mouse Studies Show

Benefits Seen in Mice Only — Never in People

Every benefit below was seen in mice — obese mice, heart-failure mice, ageing mice — or in cells in a dish. None of it has been shown in a person, because no person has ever been given this compound in a published study.

Endurance — Mice

Treated mice grew more oxidative Type IIa muscle fibres and ran longer. The gain depended on ERRα.

Fat and Blood Sugar — Mice

In obese mice, energy expenditure and fatty-acid burning rose, fat mass fell, and insulin sensitivity improved — without eating less.

Heart — Mice

In heart-failure mice, pumping improved and survival rose, driven mainly through ERRγ.

Ageing Kidneys — Mice

In 21-month-old mice, eight weeks of treatment reversed age-related kidney damage, mitochondrial failure and inflammation.

The Closest Thing to Human Evidence

Muscle cells grown from biopsies of inactive women having hip surgery were treated with SLU-PP-332 in a dish. The cells raised markers of mitochondrial and exercise response and showed less oxidative stress and fewer signs of ageing. The women themselves were never given the compound.

No Established Human Dose

No Human Dose Exists by Any Route

SLU-PP-332 has never been given to people in any published study — so there is no tested human dose, by any route, and no honest way to print one. No clinical trial is registered, and no regulator anywhere has approved it. Anything sold as a human dose for this compound is a guess, not a finding.

Safety & Sport

Named on the WADA 2027 Prohibited List

Banned in Sport, by Name

The World Anti-Doping Agency lists SLU-PP-332 and SLU-PP-915 by name on the 2027 Prohibited List, section S4.4.1 — metabolic modulators acting on oxidative metabolism. The ban is prohibited at all times and takes effect January 1, 2027. In 2026 the compound is already covered by the S0 catch-all for unapproved substances.

Already Detectable

Anti-doping laboratories have characterised the molecule and mapped its breakdown products — 9 metabolites in one study, 22 in another — so testing can find it. Its reputation has moved faster than its evidence; sport moved first.

What Safety Data Exists

Only animal and cell data. A 2026 systematic review reports no evident toxicity in the obesity mouse models, and heart-failure mice kept normal heart size while pumping improved. That is the whole of the safety record.

What Is Not Known

Its effects on the human heart, liver and kidneys are unknown. The animal treatments lasted weeks, in controlled laboratory settings — nothing about long-term use in people can be concluded from them.

Legal Status

A Research Chemical, Approved Nowhere

United States

Research chemical — never approved. Drugs@FDA holds no application for SLU-PP-332, and ClinicalTrials.gov lists no study of it (both searched November 20, 2026). It has no approval as a drug for any use.

Sport (WADA)

Prohibited. Covered by the S0 catch-all in 2026, and named outright on the 2027 Prohibited List at S4.4.1, in force January 1, 2027 — prohibited at all times, in and out of competition.

Products sold for “research use only” are not made or tested for people. A vial's label is a seller's claim, not evidence — no published independent test of products sold under this name is on record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Five Straight Answers

Is SLU-PP-332 a peptide?

No. It is a small synthetic molecule with no amino acids — C18H14N2O2. It is catalogued with peptides because it is sold beside them.

What does it do?

It switches on the estrogen-related receptors. In mice that raised endurance fibres and running time, increased energy use and cut fat in obesity, and protected the heart and ageing kidneys.

Has it been tested in people?

No. The only human material ever studied is muscle cells grown in a dish, and no clinical trial is registered. A 2026 review concludes that trials are needed to confirm whether it works — or is safe — in humans.

Is it banned in sport?

Yes — by name from January 1, 2027, under WADA S4.4.1 metabolic modulators, and already covered by the S0 catch-all before that. Anti-doping labs can already detect its metabolites.

Is it approved for anything?

No. No regulator has approved it, no drug application exists, and no study has given it to a person.
