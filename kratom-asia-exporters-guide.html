🌿 The Exporters

🛫 The Route

How a direct purchase actually travels

Most of the journey is logistics and paper. The fourth box — the border — is the one that decides whether a direct purchase was a bargain or a total loss, which is why the next section matters more than any price on this page.

⚖️ Import Reality

The border is the price nobody lists

The FDA has never approved kratom for any use. It treats kratom as an unlawful dietary ingredient and an unsafe food additive, and it detains imports under Import Alert 54-15 (last revised August 17, 2026) — an alert that lets officers hold a shipment at the border without physically examining it. That single fact shapes everything about buying direct:

  • The loss lands where the seller's policy puts it. When a shipment is detained, somebody eats the full value of the goods and the freight — and the seller's refund policy, read before wiring, decides who. Green Harmony states it plainly: seizure means no refund. Sulawesi, per its review record, refunds or replaces. Same border, opposite outcomes.
  • “Door-to-door” is a service, not a shield. An exporter can handle brokerage and paperwork — ONE Kratom builds its whole offer on it — but detention is a federal action. No contract term repeals an import alert.
  • This is why US vendors import the way they do. Established importers work through customs brokers and importers of record, keep documentation identical shipment after shipment — COA, phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin — and move volume by scheduled air and sea freight. The wholesale markup David's usual vendors charge is, in large part, the price of that machinery.
  • Warehouses are the other answer. Nusa Botanicals and Green Harmony both stock product stateside. A US-warehouse kilo costs more per kilo and carries no border risk at all — for a first test of a new supplier, that trade is often worth it.
  • The Indonesian side has paperwork too. A legitimate exporter holds Exporter Registration (ET) and an Export Permit (PE), and state lab analysis (Sucofindo) sits inside Indonesian licensing. A seller who cannot show ET/PE documentation is not an exporter of record, whatever the website says.
  • Leaf is not the current federal target. The pending federal scheduling action aims at concentrated 7-OH products above set thresholds; natural leaf below those thresholds sits expressly outside it — the full picture is tracked in the Kratom Legality by State guide. Import detention under 54-15, however, applies to kratom shipments as they arrive, leaf included.

✅ Safe First Purchase

Six steps that keep a first direct buy small, documented, and provable

1

Sample before scale

ONE Kratom ships a free 100 g sample with a full alkaloid report; Kratom Near encourages sample purchases; Sulawesi's 2-item minimum works as a test buy. Nobody reputable refuses a small first step.

2

Get the batch COA before any wire

The certificate for your batch — mitragynine, heavy metals, microbials — not a sample report from last year. Ranah publishes sample COAs openly and supplies batch COAs per shipment; Kratom Near includes the COA free with every purchase.

3

Split the payment

50/50 is the honest shape of this market: Ranah's standard wire terms are 50/50, and ONE Kratom takes half down with the balance after delivery. Full prepayment to a first-time seller is how money disappears.

4

Test it again on arrival

An independent US lab panel on the landed goods: heavy metals (Arsenic As · Lead Pb · Cadmium Cd · Mercury Hg), salmonella and microbials, and mitragynine percentage. The exporter's COA is a claim; the arrival test is the check.

5

Match the paper to the bags

Invoice, packing list, phytosanitary certificate, and COA should all name the same lot that actually lands. A batch number that changes between documents is a stop sign.

6

Scale on evidence

The second purchase gets bigger only after the first has landed clean — delivered, documented, and tested. Two clean arrivals is a supplier; one is an anecdote.
