⚖️ The Short Answer

Kratom is legal in most of the United States — but the map is splitting three ways. 11 states ban it outright, 20 states regulate it under Kratom Consumer Protection Acts and similar laws (age limits, labeling, testing, seller registration), and it is legal with no state framework in 19 states and D.C. The fastest-moving fight is not over the leaf at all — it is over 7-OH, the concentrated alkaloid sold in shots, gummies and tablets, which a growing list of states — and now the DEA — treat as a drug of its own. Purple diamonds on the map mark the states with a separate 7-OH rule.

🏛️ The Federal Picture
  • Kratom leaf and mitragynine are NOT federally scheduled. The DEA moved to schedule kratom in 2016 and withdrew the proposal after public and congressional pushback.
  • Since August 26, 2026, three lab-made kratom relatives — mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15 and MGM-16 — are temporary Schedule I substances (through August 2028).
  • Concentrated 7-OH is next: the DEA's October 14, 2026 notice of intent (91 FR 65370) would place 7-OH above a threshold — more than 1 mg per gram, or a mitragynine-to-7-OH ratio under 100:1 — in Schedule I. The order can publish any day on or after November 13, 2026 and takes effect the day it appears. As of November 16, 2026 it had not yet appeared. Natural leaf below the threshold is expressly outside the action.
  • The FDA has never approved kratom for any use, treats it as an unlawful dietary ingredient and unsafe food additive, and detains imports under Import Alert 54-15 (last revised August 17, 2026). FDA and HHS formally recommended scheduling concentrated 7-OH on July 29, 2025.

📖 How to Read the Map
  • Banned — kratom or its alkaloids are scheduled / prohibited statewide.
  • Regulated — legal to sell under a consumer-protection law: age floor, labeling, testing and/or registration.
  • Legal — no statewide ban and no consumer-protection framework.
  • 7-OH rule — a separate law targets concentrated / synthetic 7-OH even where leaf is legal.

⚠️ Local Bans & Traps
  • Denver, Colorado bans kratom sales citywide even though Colorado regulates it statewide.
  • Sarasota County, Florida bans it inside an otherwise regulated state; San Diego, Oceanside and Newport Beach, California ban it locally too.
  • California trap: no state statute — but the state health department treats kratom sold for human consumption as unlawful, so most retailers will not ship there.
  • Spokane, Washington banned sales in March 2026; some Mississippi counties and New York counties (Rensselaer, Oneida) have local bans as well.
  • Travel rule: possession laws follow the state you are standing in — a legal-state purchase is still contraband across a banned-state line.
👀 Watch List — What Changes Next
  • Kentucky — July 1, 2027: a 2026 law (HB 757) ends all kratom sales statewide on that date; possession is not criminalized. Kentucky is regulated until then.
  • Federal 7-OH order — any day: the DEA temporary scheduling order for high-potency 7-OH and MP could publish any weekday on or after November 13, 2026. Natural leaf below the thresholds is expressly excluded.
  • North Dakota — 2027: licensed 21+ sales of natural kratom begin once the Attorney General’s licensing rules are finished (expected around March 2027); sales stay prohibited until then.

  • Pending ban bills: Michigan (passed the House), Hawaii and Iowa have live ban proposals — all three states are legal today.
